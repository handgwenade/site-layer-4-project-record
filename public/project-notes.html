Energy and project research questions

The utility research targets are service/delivery point and grid demand, signed HILA and MCHIL, actual FCA costs and filing, evaluation results, due notices and valid collateral, Serve Ready Notice, and lawful public disclosure. None is proved by the template. The September 18 search and initial September 20 review’s access failures remain historical observations. The September 20 follow-up search of September 9–20 ER26-1929 filings, all subdockets, returned only the filing and notice; no later acceptance order appeared. Both erratum bodies were read and concern Agilitas and RWE complaint dockets, not a changed Tri-State deadline. See E01–E11 for status and Take action for instructions and the exact search boundary. (EERR1, EERR2)

  1. Confirm service and stage: Which utility would serve Site Layer 4, at which delivery point, and on which interconnection? Has a HILT participation package been submitted or evaluated? Establish whether the Basin/Eastern Interconnection provision applies.
  2. Utility agreement (HILA): Ask the serving cooperative and Tri-State whether an executed Site Layer 4 agreement exists; seek its date, parties, service schedule, load, guarantees, and departures from the standard form. Standard HILAs need not be individually filed at FERC (§5.3.1, PDF page 9).
  3. Customer contract (MCHIL): Establish whether the cooperative and Site Layer 4 customer have signed. Seek the terms assigning upgrade costs, minimum payments, security, and liability for delay, lower demand, cancellation, or closure. The August order, paragraphs 66–68 and 75, does not establish a required set of retail terms or prove those costs have been passed to Site Layer 4.
  4. Facilities Construction Agreement: Seek the facilities list, estimates, payer, security, and treatment of SPP network upgrades. Section 6.2 calls for filing an executed agreement within 15 business days of receipt, or an unexecuted agreement if the parties cannot agree. Search FERC using the identified parties and project names, beyond docket ER26-1929 alone (PDF page 13).
  5. Evaluation and financial protection: Obtain Site Layer 4’s load forecasts by phase, economic-test assumptions and outcome, cost estimates, security calculation, and evidence of any posted guarantee. Identify who pays if forecasts are wrong. The template’s security formula is not proof of a Site Layer 4 deposit.
  6. Later FERC action: Recheck ER26-1929, including -002, for an acceptance order or further changes. The September 18 search, with filed dates September 9–18 and no subdocket restriction, returned only notice 20260909-3058 and filing 20260909-5123. The notice references errata 20260910-3111 and 20260910-3113; their relevance has not been established. FERC eLibrary search.
  7. Public access: Ask what agreements, studies, and cost allocations can be released, including redacted copies or public summaries where appropriate. Record any stated reason for withholding; confidentiality has not been established for particular Site Layer 4 documents.
  8. Community safeguards: Separately establish enforceable provisions for water, emissions, noise, roads, services, decommissioning, and expansion. Utility financial security does not itself establish a county impact-assistance fund or cleanup bond.
  9. Ownership and scale: Identify the entities owning or operating each component, data-center demand, and on-site generation by phase and at full buildout.
